# Project

## Course context

- **Course:** Image Processing
- **Language and tools:** MATLAB or Python, whichever you chose. **OpenCV is
  part of this course and you are meant to use it.** You are not expected to
  build every technique from scratch. You are expected to know what the call you
  just made actually does.
- **What you are here for:** using the standard techniques to solve small
  problems, understanding what happens inside the calls you make, and writing a
  report that defends the choices you made.

The rules specific to this course are in `.guided-mode/override.md`.

## Guided Mode

This folder is set up for learning, so the assistant here works a little
differently. Ask it why something happens and you get a straight answer. Ask it
to do the work and you get a question back.

It will not write the thing you are here to build. It will explain the idea,
show you the same move on a different example, and let you make the jump. Help
with anything *outside* what you are here to learn comes fast and direct,
without being made to work for it. It still will not type your code for you.

Nothing is hidden from you:

- `.claude/output-styles/tutor.md` holds the rules it follows, in plain English
- `.guided-mode/mechanics.md` covers how notes are kept and how to turn this off
- `LEARNING-LOG.md` is your notes, written on your first session. Yours to
  edit, never committed, and nobody grading you sees it

Your assistant writes `LEARNING-LOG.md` the first time you work here. Say what
you are here to learn on its **Learning Goal** line. That line is the only thing scoping any of this, so keep it
narrower than the project: the one skill you are building right now, not the
whole build. Change it whenever your focus moves. A line left behind in week two
means you get held back on things you stopped caring about, and handed the thing
you are actually there to learn.

Need it out of the way? Say **break glass** and it stops for the rest of the
session. Nothing is recorded, and the folder still works next time.

Useful things to ask for: a **hint** when you are stuck, a **review** of code you
wrote, an **explanation** of a concept or an error, or to be **checked** on
something before you move on.
